# Welcome to {panel}

This guide is for the people who work in {panel} every day: editors who write and publish content, and site managers who look after the structure, the settings and the team. It explains how the panel is organised and walks through each task. You don't need to know anything about code.

What you see in the panel depends on your role. If this guide mentions a screen you can't find, your role doesn't include it: ask a site manager.

## Signing in

Open the panel's address in your browser. It's your site's address followed by **/admin** (for example `https://example.com/admin`), unless your developer chose another.

1. On the **Log in to {panel}** screen, type your **Email address** and **Password**.
2. Tick **Remember me** on your own computer to stay signed in.
3. Click **Log in**.

If the panel says your email or password doesn't match although you're sure of them, your account may not have been given access to the panel yet (the panel doesn't say which, so nobody can use it to test passwords). Ask a site manager.

After several wrong passwords the panel makes you wait before you can try again. This protects your account from people guessing passwords.

### The login code

Your site may ask for a second step after the password (two-factor login). The **Check your email** screen appears, and a 6-digit code is sent to your email address.

1. Open the email and type the code into **Login code**. The code works for 10 minutes.
2. Click **Log in**.

No email? Look in your spam folder, then click **Send a new code** (you can ask for one once a minute) and use the code from the newest email. **Back to log in** starts again.

### Forgot your password?

1. On the login screen, click **Forgot password?**.
2. Type your **Email address** and click **Email the reset link**.
3. Open the email and follow the link.
4. Type a **New password**, repeat it in **Confirm the new password**, and click **Save the new password**.

Choosing a new password also signs you out on other computers where you ticked *Remember me*.

New passwords follow your site's rules, usually 12 characters or more with upper and lower case letters, a number and a symbol; a checklist under the password ticks each rule as you type. A password that has appeared in a known data leak is refused, even if it looks strong.

If your password no longer meets the rules, the panel asks you to **Choose a stronger password** right after you sign in: type a new one twice and click **Save the new password**, and you carry on where you were going. **Log out** under it leaves without changing it.

### Your account

To change your own name, email address or password, click your name at the foot of the sidebar and choose **Your account**. A new email address or password needs your **Current password**, and a new password is typed twice. Your roles are changed by a site manager.

New to {panel}? Your site manager may have sent you an invitation: follow **Choose your password** in the email, type your password twice and log in. The link works for a limited time; after that, use **Forgot password?**.

## Finding your way around

### The sidebar

The sidebar on the left lists everything your role allows, in groups:

- **Dashboard** and **Media** at the top.
- **Content**: what you write every day, such as entries, terms, globals, menus, comments and form submissions.
- **Structure**: how the content is set up (collections, blueprints, taxonomies, forms…), for site managers.
- **Administration**: users and roles, settings, the activity log and AI access.
- **Help**: this guide, and the developer docs for the people who build the site.

The top of the sidebar shows the panel's name and logo; click it to return to the dashboard. The button at the top left of the page shrinks the sidebar to icons (hover an icon to see its name). On a phone, the same button opens the sidebar.

### Go to…

Press **Ctrl K** (Windows) or **⌘ K** (Mac), or click **Go to…** in the header, to jump anywhere: type part of a page's name, or the title of an entry, and press Enter.

### Breadcrumbs and back buttons

The header shows where you are, such as **Blog › My first post**. Click an earlier part to go back up. Editing screens also have a back arrow next to their title.

### The user menu

Your name and email address are at the bottom of the sidebar. Click them for:

- **Light**, **Dark** or **System** appearance (System follows your computer).
- **Log out**.

## Working safely

- **Saving**: editing screens say *Unsaved changes* while you have some, and ask before you leave a page without saving. **Ctrl S** (Windows) or **⌘ S** (Mac) saves.
- **One person at a time**: when a colleague is already editing something, the panel tells you who and since when. Choose **Go back**, **View only** (you can read but not change it), or **Take over**. Taking over sends them back; their unsaved changes are kept, and the next person to open the item sees them with **Load into the form** or **Discard**.
- **Someone saved before you**: if a colleague saved the same item after you opened it, saving tells you so. **Load theirs** shows their version (your changes are lost); **Save mine anyway** replaces theirs with yours.
- **Pages you can't open**: the panel shows a friendly page instead of an error when a page doesn't exist or your role doesn't allow it.

## The words used in this guide

- **Entries** are the pages, articles, products and other items of the website. Each belongs to a **collection** (*Pages*, *Blog*…).
- **Fields** are the boxes you fill in for an entry: a title, a summary, an image. A **blueprint** decides which fields a collection has.
- **Terms** group entries by topic, such as *News* in a **taxonomy** called *Categories*.
- **Globals** are values the whole site shares, such as contact details.
- **Menus** are the website's navigation.
- **Media** are your uploaded files: images, documents, videos.
